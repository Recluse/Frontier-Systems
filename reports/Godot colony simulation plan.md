# Build a Trustworthy Living Solar Frontier

Build this as a **desktop-first, server-authoritative engineering sandbox** that grows from one cooperative habitat into a persistent network of Moon and Mars colonies and ships. The recommended Godot renderer is **Forward+** for the initial desktop client; keep renderer-specific effects out of gameplay and make a measured Mobile/Compatibility support decision at the hardware gate. The core design should make engineering systems, a mass-balanced extraction-to-recycling lifecycle, reliable shared-world state, versioned blueprints, player-created automation, and optional agent-driven worlds the product, while procedural industrial art and clear functional interfaces control the art burden. Use PHP for the surrounding control plane, persistence, LLM and agent gateways, social integration, admin tools, reusable service logic, and PSR-4 contracts; keep Godot responsible for real-time presentation and the authoritative physical tick. Establish profiling from the first prototype across headless servers, clients, PHP workers, and native extensions, with repeatable pre-runtime benchmarks, safe live capture, and post-run regression reports. Treat Discord as the social interface, using the native Social SDK in the client and DiscordPHP on trusted backend services; the game’s own server and database remain authoritative for identity, permissions, programs, and world state. Let local or remote LLMs propose bounded actions, create draft programs, or control agents asynchronously; never grant generated text privileged code execution or direct world-state access. Package genuinely reusable first-party tools and content as standalone MIT Godot products when the game has validated them; treat that income as a supporting funding stream, not a dependency for core development. The ambition is feasible only through acceptance gates: first prove one complete habitat and material loop, then add persistence, social/agent features, automation, orbital industry, and interplanetary expansion when each layer meets measured reliability and performance goals.

## Prove the game with one habitat before building planets

### Design contract

The player loop is **prospect → extract → refine → build → operate → diagnose → repair → automate → expand**. Players cooperate in a persistent shared world, taking practical roles such as mining, fabrication, construction, operations, maintenance, science, and logistics without locking players out of general play. The simulation should make systems interact: power shortages affect pumps and heaters; pressure, temperature, airlocks, and life support depend on devices and operator decisions; resource extraction and salvage feed refining and construction; and failures create work that rewards communication. A remote command authority and contract economy provide shared goals, while opt-in rival operations add bounded strategic pressure. Roleplay supports this loop through crew identity, logs, shared objectives, station routines, PDAs with durable notes and planning tools, and proximity/radio communication, but never gates core engineering behind performative roleplay. The PDA and LLM planning contract is documented in [`docs/design/pda-planning-assistant.md`](../docs/design/pda-planning-assistant.md).

Treat a versioned, data-driven **blueprint system** as the reusable construction contract for habitat modules, machines, tools, rovers, spacecraft, stations, and colony infrastructure. Keep blueprint definitions, construction jobs, and spawned runtime assemblies as separate records; use composition and typed interfaces so a new object is assembled from capabilities rather than added as a one-off class. This gives future freeform building a stable foundation and lets NPCs, players, automation, persistence, and LLM tools operate on the same domain model.

Build the camera modes over the same player body and interaction model. First person is the precise maintenance and operation view; third person is an optional navigation and situational-awareness view. Both must use the same server-validated interaction commands, collision, inventory, and access rules. The first playable slice should include one small pressurized habitat, one exterior work area, an airlock, a power source and load, a pressure sensor and valve, a small inventory/construction loop, and two connected clients. Ship interiors, rovers, and orbital scenes follow after that slice proves worthwhile.

The design target is realistic **engineering behavior**, not a promise to reproduce every physical process at scientific fidelity. Define each subsystem’s assumptions, units, update cadence, and failure modes. Prefer a legible, testable pressure and power model with visible causes and consequences over a collection of opaque “realistic” numbers. Make equipment diagrams, readable labels, state indicators, and diagnostics carry complexity; defer advanced character animation and bespoke cinematic assets.

Treat ships and machines as repairable, salvageable assemblies: damage normally degrades or disables components while preserving their stable IDs and material value. Zero integrity is a recoverable salvage state, not automatic deletion or occupant death. Share typed pressure-network contracts across gases and liquids, with gas mixtures and compressible flow, liquid transfer and hydrostatic head, leaks, pumps, valves, and measurable operating limits. Exploration should connect habitable interiors to EVA suits and purpose-built small craft; crews can dock ships, stations, and craft through explicit approach/capture/seal/transfer/release states, and can operate cyborgs or instruments remotely through powered, authorized networks. See [`docs/design/survival-pressure-and-recovery.md`](../docs/design/survival-pressure-and-recovery.md) for the first-party design contract and implementation order.

Security must be interactive rather than a binary permission gate. The first airlock runtime slice supports server-context tool-gated electrical, mechanical, and combined jobs, actor-scoped temporary access to one actuator, controller alarms, mechanical latch release, and persisted tamper history. Production inventory, spatial access, repair consequences, and pressure-force mechanical overrides remain future work. Door commands still use the authoritative actuator limits and pressure interlocks. See [`docs/design/access-control-and-bypass.md`](../docs/design/access-control-and-bypass.md).

### Modular control panels and machine signals

Control panels and machinery use replaceable, compatible modules for radio reception/transmission, buttons, sensors, relays/logic, timers, device actions, alarms, and messages. A tuned radio receiver can pulse a wired door-lock adapter or an armed in-game trigger device; each module declares its ports, interface, power, compatible machine families, and registered behavior. Unsecured broadcasts can be spoofed through in-game equipment, while configured coded/authenticated receivers enforce their own rules. The first definition/slot/port contracts are implemented; live module installations and bounded fixed-tick signal routing are later milestones. See [Modular controls and machine signals](../docs/design/modular-controls-and-signals.md).

Require functioning installed components per operation. A PDA may accept a portable transceiver while a stock door receiver socket accepts only its compatible receiver family. Removing the receiver disables radio control, while a mechanically intact unlocked door may still move manually with a suitable tool. The prototype now implements crowbar door commands with independent interaction permission, physical latch/tool/force checks, and pressure interlocks; powered commands require control power. Per-function requirement records and device-specific slot fixtures codify partial functionality instead of marking the entire assembly unusable when one component is missing.

Those stock sockets can be upgraded through construction/refit jobs that replace a compatible control board with a larger or more general slot layout. The new board planner retains fitted modules, checks explicit slot moves, and rejects silent loss of occupants. Physical fit is separate from functionality: missing host services leave a mounted module dormant, and physically attachable nonsensical wiring remains buildable with an inert-route diagnostic. An expanded door board can accept an igniter in a general utility bay; its pulse-triggered local spark can affect reachable combustible gas through the shared ignition/atmosphere model. Board work, live module routing, and combustion remain future runtime milestones; board/slot/igniter definitions and upgrade planning are implemented now.

### Player identity, bodies, cyborgs, and station AI

Separate the persistent player principal from the character mind/controller and its current body or machine embodiment. A player may be a human or other organic species, a constructed cyborg, or the station AI, while permissions, ownership, bans, and account history remain attached to the same stable player principal. Model species physiology and body traits as composable data/capabilities rather than branching the whole simulation into species-specific classes. Keep body records, mind/embodiment links, life state, implants, inventories, and control leases versioned and persistent. A mind can control only one active embodiment at a time; transfers are atomic, audited server transactions that revoke the old control lease before granting the new one, preventing duplicate active copies of a player.

Keep explicit records such as `PlayerPrincipal`, `MindState`, `BodyInstance`, `EmbodimentLease`, `SpeciesDefinition`, `CyborgLoadout`, `LawSetRevision`, and `AgentDelegation`. Body state owns physical condition, installed implants/modules, location, and inventory; mind state owns player-controlled identity/continuity; the lease names the single current control route and its expiry/revocation reason. The simulation server validates and commits embodiment changes; PHP exposes PSR-4 identity/character DTOs and durable lifecycle contracts without becoming a synchronous dependency for movement or machine ticks.

Support two recovery paths for an organic player's dead character. If the brain remains recoverable, a valid operation can implant it into a constructed cyborg and resume that same player identity in the cyborg body. Otherwise, an available clone process can grow a compatible organic body and let the dead player reclaim their identity when the clone is ready. Preserve a clear distinction between a body/corpse, stored biological material, the player's persistent identity, and the active control lease; define eligibility, resource cost, timing, interruption, and failure states. A player must explicitly accept/complete the transfer, and a successful brain implant or clone activation invalidates other pending embodiment claims for that identity. Construction, access, and repair use the same blueprint, material, capability, and server validation systems as other machinery.

Cyborgs are configurable assemblies with typed module slots. Installed modules add a declared set of registered capabilities—such as movement, manipulation, construction, diagnostics, communications, or medical tools—while chassis policy and missing/damaged modules can restrict them. The server checks the active module manifest and actor permissions on every world action; a client UI limitation or law text alone is never the security boundary. Module compatibility, power draw, capacity, damage, maintenance, and installation costs belong in the blueprint/assembly model, so distinct cyborg roles emerge from loadouts instead of hard-coded character subclasses.

Treat a Station AI as a player embodiment with an explicit network of sensors, interfaces, and controllable devices. The AI can inspect or act only through reachable, powered, authorized endpoints, with communications outages, network partitions, damaged sensors, and access controls producing visible limits. Configurable lawsets are versioned, ordered policy data evaluated by a trusted law/policy engine; they guide decisions and roleplay but cannot override server ACLs, physical interlocks, player consent/approval requirements, or safety rules. Restrict lawset editing to authorized in-world procedures or administrators, and journal every change and consequential AI action.

Allow a player to delegate a cyborg or Station AI embodiment to an agentic controller, including a local or remote LLM. The player selects the delegated scope and can reclaim direct control immediately. The agent uses asynchronous observe/plan/action cycles and the same typed command tools, module capabilities, network reach, access checks, safety interlocks, budgets, audit trail, and confirmations as a human; it never receives raw world-state write access, arbitrary code execution, credentials, or authority from generated law text. Provider delays, timeouts, disconnects, or rate limits leave the embodiment in a defined safe/idle state and preserve human takeover. Keep this optional AI-control mode separate from routine NPC simulation and from the character's persistent identity.

### Versioned blueprints for modular and freeform construction

Use three distinct concepts: an immutable, versioned `BlueprintDefinition` describes a buildable design; a `ConstructionSite` records its placement, bill of materials, progress, assigned work, and unresolved requirements; and a persistent `AssemblyInstance` is the constructed object with stable part IDs, mutable operational state, ownership, and location. Blueprints are authoring and interchange data, not live scene trees. Godot nodes are runtime views/physics adapters keyed by assembly and part IDs, not the canonical definition or save format.

Represent designs as a typed graph of parts and connections. Start with grid/snap placement and explicit attachment anchors; support measured free placement and nested subassemblies after the first end-to-end assembly is validated. Every part and edge receives a stable local ID, transform, and declared type. Standard connector types cover structural attachment, power, pressure/atmosphere, fluid, data, and mechanical force/torque. The validator checks graph integrity, connector compatibility and units, structural support, access/clearance, and required subsystem capacity before a build is accepted or energized. Placement freedom should come from combining reusable parts and changing transforms, not inventing special-case behavior for every complete vehicle or building.

Ship blueprints use that same graph as a modular structural grid: hull/frame sections, decks and compartments connect to functional components and larger subassemblies through stable grid cells, anchors, and ports. Preserve discrete component IDs for construction, damage, repair, upgrades, and salvage without making every block an independent high-cost physics body. Derive ship-wide mass, center of mass, inertia, thrust/torque margin, power/fuel balance, heat rejection, pressure zones, and docking compatibility from the installed components and layout. Keep the simulator's fidelity explicit and profile aggregation strategies before increasing physical detail.

A `BlueprintProjector` renders a non-authoritative ghost of a versioned design at a selected build transform or shipyard berth. It visualizes collision/clearance, missing/changed parts, connector and structural warnings, construction stages, and a material bill of materials. Comparing a projector plan to an existing ship creates an explicit, reviewable blueprint diff; the preview never spawns an assembly, reserves resources, or edits ship state until the server validates and accepts the resulting construction/refit job.

Each versioned definition should carry the fields below. Use a shared JSON Schema (or equivalent language-neutral schema) and fixtures so PHP tools, Godot, import/export, and future mod tools agree on the contract.

| Blueprint area | Contract data |
|---|---|
| Identity and provenance | Stable blueprint ID, semantic version, display/category metadata, author, license, origin/source hashes, compatibility range, tags, and changelog. |
| Assembly graph | Part-template IDs and versions, stable local IDs, parent/subassembly links, transforms, footprint/clearance, snap anchors, connector endpoints, and optional placement constraints. |
| Construction | Material bill of materials, recipes, tools, skill/crew requirements, access rules, staged work/time, construction method, prerequisite technology/contracts, and salvage/recovery policy. |
| Operation | Required/optional power, fuel, coolant, pressure, atmosphere, crew, environment, calibration, maintenance, consumables, and startup/shutdown procedures. |
| Ports and capabilities | Typed connector schemas with units/rating/direction plus declared capabilities such as storage, sensing, actuation, power generation, propulsion, processing, life support, or programmable control. |
| Machine control modules | Compatible slots, definition revisions, installed module IDs, wired/radio ports, connections, receiver settings, and scoped action adapters. |
| Limits and failures | Operating envelopes, load/pressure/temperature ratings, wear model, fault states, safe states, interlocks, degradation, and repair requirements. |
| Presentation and editing | MIT-cleared mesh/material/audio references or procedural generator IDs, collision/interaction hints, labels, module icons, editor grouping, snapping aids, and diagnostic diagram metadata. |
| Evolution | Schema version, explicit migration steps, compatibility policy, and validation fixtures for old saved instances. |

Definitions are declarative and never contain arbitrary executable code. A capability name only selects an implementation from a server-owned, versioned registry; it grants no access by itself. Behavior belongs to trusted simulation components or to the bounded in-game DSL/VM, whose permissions are independently derived from the actor and device ACL. Imported blueprints cannot execute scripts, load libraries, create new capabilities, or widen permissions. Validate size/part counts, dependency depth, ports, units, resource budgets, hashes, migration paths, and license provenance at import and again at server placement. Shipped first-party definitions, schema tooling, examples, and procedural generators must be MIT-licensable; user-authored blueprints remain attributable to their author unless they explicitly contribute them under the project's MIT terms.

Keep authoring/editor DTOs, the canonical interchange schema, construction planning, and runtime domain records behind separate interfaces. In PHP, make the shared schema/validator a small PSR-4 Composer package with versioned DTOs, migration/validation services, and contract fixtures. In Godot, parse into typed domain records and hand plans to the authoritative construction service; do not let UI/editor code or a PHP round trip apply physical state. The first proof is one airlock/power/storage module built from the same data path. Broaden to player-designed nested assemblies only after save/reload, server validation, multiplayer placement, and load budgets pass.

### Resource lifecycle, recycling, refining, and fabrication

Make material flow a first-class simulation domain shared by geology, salvage, inventories, construction sites, shipyards, and the economy. Every manufactured part and assembly must declare its complete material composition and supported disassembly/recovery path; nested designs resolve from assembly to subassembly, part, and material constituents, with no opaque “free mass” item exempt from the ledger. No object is intrinsically unrecyclable: every assembly can be disassembled through its part graph, and every part can be repaired, reused, separated, or reprocessed into lower-level material batches. “Break down to basics” is a tracked engineering process, not instant deletion and recreation. Early fidelity should track a useful set of elements/material families, composition, phase, purity, contamination, structural condition, mass, temperature, provenance, and recoverability; only add detailed isotope/chemistry behavior where a gameplay system needs it.

Use versioned records such as `MaterialDefinition`, `MaterialBatch`, `ResourceDeposit`, `ProcessDefinition`, `ProcessJob`, and `RecoveryRecord`. Materials have stable IDs and declared composition/properties; batches carry measured quantity, composition or grade, contaminants, state, source, owner, and location. Process definitions declare input and output batch types, expected yields and byproducts, consumables/catalysts, tools, power/energy, temperature/pressure/atmosphere windows, time, operator requirements, failure modes, and waste handling. Keep quantities in canonical units and make each supported process conserve tracked element/mass quantities within explicit rounding tolerances; any unrecovered material becomes a named contaminant, slag, tailings, gas, or damaged batch rather than disappearing. Track dissipated energy separately as heat/work. Ordinary fabrication does not perform nuclear transmutation. Batches, not individual particles, are the unit of storage and simulation.

Apply one material ledger to all acquisition and recovery paths: prospecting and sampling; surface/regolith excavation; drilling and deep mining; ice/volatile extraction; asteroid/meteor collection; and salvage from derelicts, damaged ships, machinery, construction mistakes, and obsolete equipment. Deposits are finite, spatially placed, and generated from versioned geological profiles; tools, sensor resolution, drill capability, terrain, power, dust, suit limits, and contamination change what can be extracted and at what grade. Salvage first offers intact component recovery when the part is accessible and sufficiently sound; breaking that part into feedstock requires a suitable dismantling/recycling process and yields condition- and material-dependent outputs. Preserve ownership, claim, contract credit, and provenance for recovered items and batches.

Refining and combination use player-operable process chains rather than magic recipe buttons: sorting/separation, crushing, washing, drying, reduction, smelting, alloying, gas/liquid handling, casting, powder production, and later specialized chemical/electrochemical steps. Recipes are versioned declarative process definitions with mass balance, quality bounds, tolerances, energy requirements, hazards, and byproducts. Let players feed different valid material batches into a research/assay workflow to discover useful compositions or process settings, then persist approved discoveries as versioned data—not executable code. The same process definition drives manual equipment, automated plant controls, in-game programs, NPC work orders, agent tools, and LLM explanations. A changed feedstock or operating parameter must change the result through the modeled yield/quality rules.

3D printing and other fabrication consume real prepared feedstock, energy, tooling, time, and machine capacity. Each printer/fabricator declares an envelope, supported feedstock forms (for example filament, powder, wire, liquid, or slurry), resolution/feature limits, material compatibility, waste/support behavior, calibration and maintenance state, and required post-processing. Printing instantiates a validated blueprint/part only when its bill of materials can be fulfilled by compatible batches and the machine meets its process window; the generated part keeps its material provenance and can later be disassembled and recycled by the same system. Use machining, casting, assembly, and additive processes as distinct methods so players choose equipment and tradeoffs rather than one universal fabricator.

Blueprint construction bills of materials should resolve to quantities and acceptable grades of actual batches, not abstract credits or an opaque “metal” counter. Construction and shipyard jobs reserve exact batch IDs/quantities, show expected products/byproducts, and release or reconcile reservations on pause, abort, salvage, or rollback. A blueprint may specify material constraints and manufacturing methods but cannot embed arbitrary process code. Replacing/removing ship components returns salvage outputs through the recovery rules, and player-run yards can feed recovered material straight into later jobs after the required inspection/refining.

Use **Take On Mars** as the core gameplay reference for hands-on resource discovery, extraction, practical survival engineering, and growing a Mars operation; extend that loop with Space Station 13-inspired mining/salvage/refining/fabrication choices, Space Engineers-style modular recovery and reconstruction, and Stationeers-style process equipment and automation. Keep our implementation, terminology, balance, data model, UI, fiction, and assets original. These games are behavioral references only: do not copy code, game assets, text, blueprints, or proprietary data. The central player promise is that a useful machine can be mined from local materials, printed/assembled, operated, repaired, recovered from a wreck, and turned into different useful equipment without a special-case recycling path. ([Take On Mars](https://store.steampowered.com/app/244030/), [SS13 making and breaking](https://wiki.ss13.co/Making_and_Breaking), [SS13 ore processing](https://wiki.ss13.co/Ore_Processing), [Stationeers](https://store.steampowered.com/app/544550/), [Space Engineers](https://store.steampowered.com/app/244850/))

### Command authority, contracts, and rival pressure

Give the colony a remote sponsor/command authority that issues finite, legible contracts for useful outcomes: maintaining system uptime, restoring a failed subsystem, rescuing a crew, delivering logistics, recovering salvage, or validating a discovery. Each contract names its required evidence, due window, shared budget, risks, and reward. Pay against verified results and service quality, not raw action counts; use diminishing returns, duplicate-work detection, and contract refresh rules to prevent players from farming repetitive chores. Contracts should create a reason to coordinate and plan around engineering constraints, not turn routine maintenance into a job checklist.

Separate the **colony treasury** from **personal pay and reputation**. Contracts disclose the budget and its division between crew funds, operations, and research before acceptance. The shared treasury pays for fuel, consumables, parts, repairs, and colony upgrades; personal pay can buy optional services or approved equipment, while reputation opens training, trust, and future contract opportunities. Keep these ledgers visible in a colony board with proposed and approved spending, current balance, escrow, costs, earned rewards, and responsible role. Prevent one player from silently draining shared funds: use role-scoped spending limits, approval thresholds, and an auditable journal. Personal progression should complement contribution, never outbid the colony’s life-support needs.

Use the remote-authority-versus-rival dynamic as a **reference pattern**, not shipped SS13 content. A rival faction can compete for contracts, exploit intelligence gaps, or stage a sabotage/espionage operation that tests detection, containment, repair, and recovery. Launch this only inside an explicitly opted-in campaign zone or bounded event with a clear start/end, permissions, visible attribution/audit, safe areas, and reversible effects. Rival activity cannot permanently destroy a non-consenting colony, steal protected account/treasury state, or bypass the same access and simulation rules players obey. Make the first event a controlled scenario that creates a repairable, recoverable engineering incident; later human-versus-human infiltration remains opt-in and equally bounded.

Use CentCom-versus-Syndicate only as internal shorthand for the inspiration. Because the game is a commercial MIT project, ship original faction names, lore, dialogue/text, art, logos, and branding unless the project has explicit rights to use SS13 material. All original shipped text and assets remain first-party MIT contributions under the project’s existing licensing policy.

### Configurable events and incidents

Make a server-authoritative, data-driven Event Director the only system that schedules or cancels world hazards. Each versioned event definition carries an ID/category, eligibility predicates, permitted trigger sources, severity and random-selection weight, per-event cooldown, maximum active count, warning/telegraph, duration and end conditions, mitigation/resolution rules, persistence policy, and cancel/recovery behavior. The director evaluates definitions against current world state and permission policy, then asks the normal simulation systems to apply typed effects; clients never write event state directly.

Store per-world master, category, and individual-event toggles plus named presets such as quiet operations, standard frontier, and opt-in campaign. A disabled master, category, or event toggle blocks every trigger; weight controls only seeded random selection, so zero weight means “never selected at random” while an authorized causal or admin trigger can still run if that event is enabled. Permit three trigger sources: seeded random scheduling; validated in-game causal events such as a declared component fault or environmental threshold; and permission-gated admin commands. Persist the world seed, selected parameters, lifecycle transitions, warnings, mitigations, and outcome in the journal so an event can recover across restart and be audited; use stored lifecycle state for recovery rather than promising bit-identical physics replay.

Support temporary electrical outages, meteor-storm windows, and an original hostile organism/biomass quarantine outbreak (working title “Ferrospore Breach”) with distinct biology, spread and containment rules, visuals, and lore; do not copy SS13 Blob naming, mechanics, art, text, or story. Add future hazards through the same definition and mitigation contract. Warn and telegraph before consequential effects, define eligible players and offline advancement policy, and scale severity to the active crew and colony progression. Default empty colonies to safe pause or harmless resolution. Random incidents do not permanently wipe a colony by default. Rival sabotage remains inside the bounded opt-in campaign/event rules above.

Expose event administration through the PHP control plane as preview, confirm, schedule, cancel, and per-world configuration actions. Preview shows the target world, proposed severity/parameters, eligible players, current event count, and likely effects without mutating state; confirm requires an authorized operator, and cancel records a reason and recovery action. A separate, higher-privilege emergency-shutdown command immediately stops new event starts and applies a safe stop to active event effects; it is not a delayed ordinary schedule/cancel request. Send these commands through the existing asynchronous Promise/Future adapter with request IDs, deadlines, and audit context. The Godot region server revalidates ACLs, world state, toggles, cooldowns, and concurrency on receipt, then applies the lifecycle transition on the simulation thread without blocking a tick.

### Renderer and engine baseline

Choose **Forward+** for a desktop-first 3D simulation because Godot describes it as its desktop renderer with the broadest rendering feature set. Mobile targets mobile-oriented hardware and also runs on desktop; Compatibility targets the widest, older hardware set and is the required renderer for web. Those are platform trade-offs, not separate gameplay designs. Keep materials, lighting, shaders, and post-processing within a documented feature budget, then run representative habitat and exterior scenes on each renderer before promising additional platforms. Godot’s renderer guide supports this recommendation and explains the compatibility choices. ([Godot: Renderers](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html))

Pin the Godot release, renderer, physics backend, tick settings, native extension toolchain, and export templates in the project. A dedicated headless server can run the same project without rendering, while a dedicated export can strip visual assets; server packaging is therefore separate from the client renderer decision. ([Godot: Dedicated server exports](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/export/exporting_for_dedicated_servers.html))

## Keep simulation authority, persistence, and social services separate

### World topology and authoritative state

Run a dedicated Godot process as the authority for each active physical region, initially one process for the first world. Clients submit **intent**—move, interact, configure a machine, edit a program—not final world state. The server authenticates the game principal, resolves stable entity IDs, checks permission, range, preconditions, rate limits, and safety interlocks, applies the transition, persists its result, and then replicates the authoritative revision. Godot’s multiplayer API does not automatically secure gameplay logic; its guidance calls for untrusted-client validation and server-authoritative decisions. Use reliable ordered delivery for durable actions and configuration, and separate frequent, replaceable movement updates from chat or other traffic where channels help. ([Godot: High-level multiplayer and secure design](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/networking/high_level_multiplayer.html))

Do not make Godot scene nodes the save format. Store stable entity IDs and versioned domain records for colony membership, devices, inventories, programs, body state, mind/embodiment links, control leases, and durable state in the game-owned persistence layer; use runtime nodes as loaded views and physics objects keyed by those IDs. Keep the player principal, embodied mind/controller, and body/assembly as distinct records with explicit ownership and transfer events. A practical first production shape is a dedicated simulation service plus a game-owned relational database, with transactional state changes, snapshots, and a recoverable event journal. Keep the storage interface behind a small service boundary so the first prototype can use a simpler local store without contaminating simulation rules. Add offline catch-up only after each subsystem defines which rates continue while a region is inactive and how bounded catch-up is performed.

### Profiling before, during, and after runtime

Make profiling a first-class subsystem across Godot clients, headless region servers, PHP services/workers, and native extensions. Define a stable profiling contract with named spans/counters and a capture schema. Correlate samples to build/commit, engine and renderer version, OS/hardware class, server/client role, world/region, scenario, entity counts, and simulation configuration so a slow capture can be reproduced. Report tick and frame p50/p95/p99/max, deadline misses, per-subsystem CPU time, main/worker thread utilization, memory/RSS and growth, Godot object/node counts, GPU/frame/render/VRAM measures, network packet/application-byte rates, replication volume, persistence latency, async queue depth/age, Promise/Future completion latency and outcomes, and dropped profile samples. Use bounded buffers and sampling; detailed traces must be opt-in or triggerable, profile export must be asynchronous, and a full collector queue drops diagnostic samples with a visible counter rather than blocking a frame, tick, or gameplay action.

Organize the workflow into three stages:

1. **Before runtime:** maintain repeatable headless and client benchmark scenarios for cold startup/import, scene/region loading, join/late-join, construction, dense machinery, atmosphere/power solvers, blueprint validation, deposit extraction, batch sorting/refining, 3D-print jobs, large salvage/recycling runs, ship grids at increasing part counts, concurrent shipyard jobs, agent-driven action bursts, refit and repair jobs, procedural generation/materialization/retirement, replication, persistence checkpoints, and representative player/entity counts. Capture baselines on named reference hardware and compare release builds against budgets. Keep budgets explicit but derive initial numbers from measurements on target hardware rather than guessing them; profile representative renderer settings separately.
2. **During runtime:** use Godot's Profiler, Monitors/custom Performance monitors, Network Profiler, Video RAM and ObjectDB tools in development, plus a project performance overlay and server metrics endpoint. Expose lightweight live summaries for authorized operators and capture triggerable traces around tick-budget violations, memory growth, queue buildup, network spikes, or player-reported hitches. Add spans for subsystem phases and async request lifecycle (enqueue, queue wait, service response, completion dispatch, revalidation/application), but never profile by logging private payloads or make profile transmission synchronous. Godot supplies profiler/debugger facilities and custom monitors; the game adds domain counters because engine-only measurements cannot explain the full station simulation. ([Godot: Profiler](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/debug/the_profiler.html), [Godot: Debugger and Network Profiler](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/debug/debugger_panel.html), [Godot: Custom performance monitors](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/debug/custom_performance_monitors.html), [Godot: ObjectDB profiler](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/debug/objectdb_profiler.html))
3. **After runtime:** save a compact manifest plus counters and optionally a sampled trace/flame profile as an inspectable artifact keyed by a capture ID. Generate a report that ranks hot paths, tick/frame outliers, queue stalls, memory/object growth, bandwidth-heavy entities, and regression versus the baseline; include the exact scenario and build metadata. Provide local report viewing and an authorized, opt-in upload path for self-hosted/development captures. Keep raw profiles behind admin/developer access with retention limits; do not collect chat/voice contents, credentials, private LLM prompts, or personally identifying account data. For PHP, profile representative requests/jobs with Xdebug's selectively triggered profiler and inspect its Cachegrind-compatible output; keep heavy instrumentation out of ordinary production requests. ([Xdebug: Profiling](https://xdebug.org/docs/profiler))

Keep the instrumentation adapter backend-neutral and version its capture schema. PHP may validate, aggregate, and render uploaded profile artifacts using PSR-4 contracts and tools, but the collection path is optional: a disconnected profiler service or full disk must never affect world simulation. Store minimal default counters locally, enable detailed sampling only for development, controlled diagnostics, or an authorized temporary capture, and count dropped data rather than allowing instrumentation overhead to hide the issue it measures.

Keep simulation domains explicit. Atmosphere, power, thermal, fluid, life-support, resource processing, and machine networks should use typed ports and authored subsystem update cadences. For the BYOND station-simulation replacement goal, prefer typed domain interfaces and composition with shallow inheritance: an `AtmosDevice` base may own identity, lifecycle, and port registration, while `Powered`, `Programmable`, `ValveActuator`, and `PressureSensor` are separate capabilities/components. Avoid a deep inheritance tree or a universal machine class full of unrelated flags. Keep domain solvers pure and independent of renderer, scene nodes, and network transport: given versioned input state and commands, a solver returns a state delta and typed events. Godot adapters present those records, resolve physics, and replicate results; PHP handles surrounding transactions and services rather than taking a per-tick dependency. Use stable entity IDs, explicit units, schema versions, and migration tests. Persist meaningful results and state transitions, not each solver intermediate. Do not assume deterministic physics lockstep: test deterministic domain-system fixtures and replay inputs where useful, while treating the physics engine as an authoritative server simulation. Pin the selected physics backend and test joint-heavy machinery and vehicles before standardizing on them. ([Godot: Physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.html), [Godot: Jolt Physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html))

Keep early atmosphere scope deliberately small: model compartments with a pressure value, a leak/flow abstraction, pressure sensors, doors, and valve/equalization rules sufficient to make airlock procedures and pressure failures understandable. Test pressure bounds, door interlocks, and safe recovery. Defer species mixtures, partial pressures, combustion chemistry, and detailed thermodynamics until the habitat, persistence, and performance gates are met; those are later, low-priority fidelity work rather than prerequisites for the first playable loop.

Use composition consistently across blueprints and simulation domains. Small interfaces such as `PowerConsumer`, `PressureBoundary`, `DataEndpoint`, `Constructible`, `Repairable`, and `Programmable` should define contracts; shallow base types should only own genuinely shared identity/lifecycle behavior. A rover, airlock, and hand tool can therefore share selected capabilities without inheriting unrelated machinery, while solvers remain testable without rendering, networking, or editor dependencies.


### PHP-first service boundaries and package layout

Use PHP aggressively where work is not inside the simulation’s frame/tick path. Keep immediate movement, collision, machine state transitions, interlocks, and replication in the authoritative Godot process; do not make a per-tick PHP request or synchronously round-trip every interaction. Put reusable account, access-policy, program-revision, persistence, AI-provider, moderation, and administration logic in PHP services and packages. Godot calls versioned PHP APIs for session bootstrap, group/access updates, durable checkpoints, program lifecycle, and LLM proposals. Cache signed/versioned policy snapshots in the region server and fail closed when stale; persist durable commands/checkpoints through an idempotent batch/write API outside every physics tick. Define whether a command needs a storage acknowledgement before it is reported as durable, and use a bounded retry/outbox with a safe-mode policy when persistence is unavailable.

Every Godot-to-PHP, Godot-to-Discord, or Godot-to-Steam request must use an asynchronous adapter with a Promise/Future-style contract. In GDScript, implement that facade with typed request/result objects, signals or coroutines, and a completion queue; Godot documents await as resuming after a signal/coroutine, and HTTPRequest exposes request-completion signals. This is a project abstraction, not a claim that Godot ships a built-in Promise class. The physics/server tick only enqueues work and consumes already-completed results; it never awaits or performs blocking I/O. Apply deadlines, cancellation, idempotency keys, bounded queues, and back-pressure per service. Marshal completions to the simulation thread, revalidate actor, permissions, device revision, and preconditions before applying any state change, and treat LLM/Discord/Steam completions as proposals or events. For persistence, journal durable commands/checkpoints in order and stop accepting durability-critical work or enter a documented safe mode before a bounded queue can overflow; never silently drop an acknowledged change. ([Godot: GDScript await](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/gdscript/gdscript_basics.html), [Godot: HTTPRequest](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_httprequest.html))

### Surface regions, ships, and expansion

Use compact, origin-centered physics sectors, not one Moon-sized or interplanetary scene. Store a separate logical location such as `body_id + surface_tile + local_pose` for a planetary site, or `ship_id + compartment + local_pose` aboard a ship. A local Godot region loads around its own origin. Planet surfaces and ship interiors can become separate server regions or simulation spaces, and transfers move stable entity/player records plus a durable state revision. Represent orbital/interplanetary movement initially as a route, elapsed time, fuel, and encounter state; load a compact flight sector only when players are aboard and the scenario needs it. This avoids putting whole-body gravity and every colony into one rigid-body world.

Godot’s single-precision guidance flags visible and physics precision problems a few kilometers from origin; its large-world-coordinate option brings a custom double-precision build, extra performance/memory cost, matching client/server requirements, and GDExtension rebuild obligations. Region-local coordinates are the safer default; adopt double precision only if a measured prototype proves an active local scene must exceed the documented range. ([Godot: 3D coordinates](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/introduction_to_3d.html), [Godot: Large world coordinates](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/physics/large_world_coordinates.html))

### Player-built ships, refits, and field repairs

Ships are persistent, player-owned `AssemblyInstance`s built from the versioned blueprint parts and staged construction sites—not fixed vehicle prefabs. Provide a build area or shipyard where crews can lay out a hull grid, add/replace components, and commit construction stages by delivering the required materials and work. Let players start from a basic frame or modify a ship they already own. Ship subsystems should be composable modules (for example propulsion, fuel/power generation and storage, radiators, life support, pressure compartments, cargo, navigation/sensors, communications, docking, and crew spaces). A design's functional envelope is derived from its installed modules and their ratings; upgrades can change capability but also mass, power draw, heat rejection, handling, maintenance, and resource needs.

Support **in-place refitting and modular expansion**. A versioned blueprint diff proposes additions, removals, moves, or replacements against the current ship revision. The server checks ownership/crew permissions, occupied spaces and stored cargo, attachment geometry, structural loads, connector compatibility, access, and the resulting mass/center-of-mass, power, pressure, and propulsion constraints before reserving materials and starting work. During upgrades, preserve the ship ID, unchanged part IDs, records, ownership, log, and inventory; require explicit handling of contents and occupants before a change can remove or isolate their space. Add standardized structural, docking, power, fluid/fuel, pressure, and data ports so a crew can attach a cargo pod, lab, habitat section, engine stage, or other compatible module instead of rebuilding the full vessel. Recompute flight and service limits after each accepted topology revision and show the impact before construction.

Make repair a physical work loop both **inside the ship** and **outside on the hull**. Track integrity and faults per repairable part/module plus shared compartment pressure/seal and connected-network state. Interior work should require access to the damaged machinery or maintenance panels, diagnosis, isolation/lockout of connected power/pressure/fluid systems, the correct tools, and replacement components. Exterior work should require an EVA-capable suit, tether/airlock route, light/communications, appropriate hand or robotic tools, and an exterior access point; expose hazards such as suit power limits, debris, radiation, spin, thermal conditions, and loss of a foothold. Repairs consume time and materials, can be interrupted, and are validated by the same server-side capability, permission, and interlock rules as other actions. Repairing a derelict can yield serviceable components for refit, but salvage status, provenance, and any claim/reward remain persistent.

### Player-run orbital shipyards and AI-assisted fabrication

Let players construct, operate, upgrade, and expand orbital shipyards from the same blueprint assemblies. A small first yard needs a structurally supported work frame, safe berth/docking interfaces, alignment/metrology sensors, powered fabrication and repair tools, component/material storage and feeds, communications, and defined crew/EVA access. Larger yards grow by attaching compatible gantries, docks, storage, power/thermal, fabrication, and habitation modules. Yard capacity is computed from installed services and ratings—berths, hull envelope, mass handling, power, heat rejection, precision, and work throughput—rather than granted by a single magic station object. Fabrication consumes actual refined batches and returns declared scrap/byproducts; recycled ship parts retain provenance and may need inspection or reprocessing before a yard can use them.

Use projectors and durable **construction job graphs** to automate physical work. A job pins the blueprint ID/version/hash, target ship/yard revision, berth and transform, diff, material reservations, prerequisites, work stages, assigned tools/agents, and applicable access/interlock policy. Server validation checks ownership/permissions, bay fit and clearance, available materials, support/load limits, power/fabrication capacity, and conflicts with occupants, traffic, or other jobs before reserving resources. Stages produce visible assembly progress—frame and attachment, subsystem fit-out, network connection, hull closure, inspection, and commissioning—using deterministic server-side tasks and the normal construction/simulation systems. Jobs can be paused, resumed, reprioritized, safely aborted, salvaged, or rolled back only according to a defined recovery policy; persist their revisions, locks, progress, and resource ledger across disconnects and restarts. Players may offer yard capacity for crew contracts, repairs, expansions, and salvage refits, creating a reason to invest in infrastructure.

Make AI control a supervised task planner over the same authorized controls. A player can assign a Station AI, cyborg, or dedicated yard agent a bounded role such as inspection, material/process planning, fault triage, or executing a reviewed construction queue. It can read the approved blueprint and yard documentation, calculate/compare bills of materials and mass balances, identify blocked stages, propose job graphs, and request permitted actions such as inspect, reserve batches, enqueue, pause, inspect, or resume. It cannot create new capability types, bypass ACLs or safety interlocks, exceed job budgets, move occupied modules, spend unbounded treasury/materials, or change ownership. Require explicit player/role approval for new ship builds, major refits, expensive reservations, module removal, pressure-boundary work, and commissioning; a player may delegate a signed job scope and spend/time limits for repeatable lower-risk steps. Log the AI principal, model/provider, authorization scope, plan revision, approvals, commands, and outcomes. LLM calls and remote services are asynchronous proposals; a deterministic job scheduler advances already authorized work without waiting on inference, and manual controls remain available if the model is offline.

Use **Space Engineers** as a high-level gameplay reference for player-built, block-connected ships/stations and manual assembly/disassembly; its Steam description emphasizes designing and piloting ships and stations, functional blocks, and survival construction. Specify an original grid, component catalog, damage model, interface, and progression; do not copy its code, art, workshop builds, block names, UI, or other game content. ([Space Engineers on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/244850/Space_Engineers/))

### Procedural bodies, discovery, and transient salvage regions

Use procedural generation to extend the persistent world without simulating every planet or asteroid continuously. Generate compact body/region records from versioned data profiles and deterministic seeds, then materialize detailed scenes only when players discover, schedule, or travel to them. A `GenerationProfile` describes a body/region family, size and terrain envelope, resource distribution, environmental conditions, threat/event tables, points of interest, access constraints, and a versioned generator. Keep the generator a pure, renderer-independent function of world seed, stable target ID, profile ID/version, and generation algorithm version; it returns validated content records, not live scene nodes or direct persistence effects. The region authority validates and commits those records before constructing the runtime view.

Persist the target ID, seed, profile/generator version, generated manifest/hash, discovery state, player/world changes, extracted resources, event outcomes, and player-owned structures. Persist mutable deltas and outcomes rather than every unchanged procedural tile or object. Unvisited candidates and unmodified temporary instances may be expired and regenerated or removed under the world’s declared retention rules. When a materialized asteroid/region is empty and eligible for retirement, the server may delete its active generated geometry/entities and retain a compact tombstone, seed/version reference, discovery/extraction ledger, and outcome history; a later regenerated opportunity gets a new stable target ID after its cooldown so farming cannot duplicate rewards. Unchanged deterministic detail can also be unloaded and rebuilt from the same versioned seed. Never delete player-built assets, claimed salvage, earned rewards, unresolved contracts, protected world history, or material state simply to save memory. Expire transient content only under a published rule, after players are clear and any extraction/claim window has resolved; if a crew is present, pause/extend the hazard timer or complete an explicit evacuation/transfer flow. Procedural retirement is a server-owned state transition with an audit record, not a client-side scene deletion.

Make body resources and threats authored, data-driven profiles rather than arbitrary random rolls. Distinct lunar, Martian, asteroid, and future-body profiles can vary geology, deposits, volatile compounds, power/communication conditions, radiation, dust, structural hazards, wildlife/biological threats, and salvage density. Bound distributions and define rarity, extraction rate, regeneration/renewal, and trade/economy effects so generation does not flood the market or invalidate existing contracts. Seeded generation must avoid duplicate stable IDs, resource deposits that violate physical constraints, inaccessible critical resources, or unwinnable scenarios. Use a progression gate: authored known sites first, a small deterministic asteroid set next, then larger configurable body catalogs and generated planetary regions only after profiling and persistence gates.

Add time-limited orbital salvage as a distinct region lifecycle coordinated with, but not conflated into, the Event Director. A derelict station or cargo structure can enter a decaying-orbit track with known orbit window, trajectory uncertainty, debris field, spin/tumble, residual power, pressure state, contamination/threat profile, and eventual atmospheric entry or disposal. The server exposes a discoverable track and an estimated salvage window; the crew can plan a launch/intercept, stabilize, dock or tether, isolate power/pressure hazards, extract cargo/components/data, and return safely. Reaching it should require appropriate ship navigation/intercept capability, EVA-rated suits and tethers, communication/power support, anchoring/docking or capture equipment, cutting/repair tools, storage capacity, and trained role coordination. Equipment requirements, hazard ratings, salvage tables, deadlines, mitigations, abort paths, and rewards all live in versioned profiles/contracts and use the same blueprint, capability, permission, and event systems as ordinary construction. The site must be recoverable or safely abandonable; avoid a timer that traps a disconnected or returning crew.

Treat **Helion** as a user-nominated gameplay reference for salvage expeditions and the desired feel of upgradeable components, repairs inside and outside a ship, and modular expansions. The Steam listing describes online co-op, roles, and space-station maps; treat the specific salvage/refit/repair mechanics in this plan as our original product requirements, not as claims verified by that listing. Record the neutral behavior lesson and our own acceptance tests in the reference manifest. Do not reuse Helion's art, maps, writing, UI, names, or other proprietary content. ([Helion on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2605880/Helion/))

The SDK supplies voice call streams and controls; positional audio remains game code. Keep voice state separate from radio membership and safety-critical simulation communication. If linked channels are supported, explain that messages from a private Discord channel become visible to every lobby member. Do not store Discord friends as a long-term game-owned friend graph. A Discord role, message, attachment, or lobby membership must not grant world permissions by itself—the game account and current colony ACL decide. ([Valve: Steam networking and SDR](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/multiplayer/networking), [Godot: High-level multiplayer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/networking/high_level_multiplayer.html))

Publish selected, game-owned progression as Steam stats/achievements through a trusted server or an explicitly validated client/server policy; Steam values are an external projection, not the canonical world or achievement record. ## Let players and LLMs automate through narrow controls

### Player scripts are a small, versioned language

Implement a purpose-built DSL compiled to typed bytecode and run it in a server-owned bounded VM. Begin with timers/events, named sensor reads, typed comparisons and branches, bounded loops over statically resolved device sets, and allowlisted actuator commands. Use explicit engineering units and stable entity IDs. Exclude arbitrary GDScript/C#, shell, imports, reflection, dynamic property access, network/filesystem access, recursion, unbounded loops, and dynamic code execution. Do not treat Godot’s `Expression` evaluator as a sandbox: it supports method calls and can receive a base object. ([Godot: Expression](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_expression.html), [Godot: Evaluating expressions](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/evaluating_expressions.html))

Each program carries a server-issued capability manifest: for example, named sensor IDs and channels, allowed actuator IDs and command ranges, event subscriptions, and rate ceilings. Resolve rights from the owner’s current game role and device ACL, then re-check them at execution so revoked roles, replaced devices, and ownership changes fail closed. Enforce independent source-size, compile, instruction-fuel, memory, loop, subscription, command, and pending-action budgets. Safety interlocks always outrank automation; player control and program conflicts receive explicit server arbitration. Persist canonical source, immutable revision, DSL/API version, source hash, owner/scope, capability manifest, and activation metadata. Compiled bytecode is a rebuildable cache. Recompile on load and pause with a clear error if migration or permissions fail.

### LLMs propose; the game server decides

Hide provider differences behind an adapter that accepts bounded conversation context, tool schemas, and deadlines. Deliver Ollama as the first local provider; add remote providers only through an explicit setting that tells the player where prompt and world context will be sent. Make local inference the zero-service-bill default; require opt-in per world/agent for remote inference, enforce request/token/concurrency and monthly spend limits, and expose estimated/actual usage to the operator. Provider tool-calling is a proposal format, not an authorization boundary. Validate every response against the complete internal schema, limit tool-loop depth, request size, output tokens, concurrency, and time, and never block simulation ticks on inference. On provider failure, leave world state unchanged and preserve manual controls. ([Ollama: Tool calling](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/tool-calling), [Ollama: Chat API](https://docs.ollama.com/api/chat))

Expose a small task-specific tool set such as `inspect_device`, `read_sensor`, `request_device_action(device_id, action_enum, typed_value)`, `inspect_material_batch`, `calculate_material_balance`, `simulate_process`, `create_program_draft`, and `validate_program`. Add shipyard tools such as `inspect_blueprint`, `calculate_materials`, `validate_shipyard_plan`, `draft_shipyard_job`, and `request_shipyard_job_action`. Never expose generic `run_code`, arbitrary method/property access, filesystem, network, database, or Discord token tools. For “open this door,” the model may propose the door and `OPEN`; trusted code then derives the authenticated player principal and checks permission, target identity, remote-control/range rules, pressure differential, power, airlock interlocks, and rate limit before using the same door state machine as a physical control. Require explicit confirmation for pressure boundaries, life-support setpoints, disabling power, cargo jettison, material batch reservations above delegated limits, destructive recycling, major ship refits, and program activation; bind confirmation to the exact action and short expiry. A confirmation cannot override a safety interlock.

For program assistance, ask the model for an unactivated DSL draft. Parse, type-check, show the source and diff, calculate its capabilities, run it in a bounded fixture/simulation, and require an authorized human to activate it. Treat chat, signage, names, logs, program comments, sensor labels, and tool responses as untrusted data that cannot change permissions. Never send credentials to the model. OWASP warns about prompt injection and excessive agency; the security boundary therefore belongs in application checks, not a trusted prompt. ([OWASP: Prompt injection prevention](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html), [OWASP GenAI: Excessive agency](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/))

### External agents and LLM-driven sandbox worlds

Support three independently configurable world control policies: `human_coop` (human-controlled crew with optional onboard assistants), `mixed` (human players plus delegated AI/cyborg/Station AI and agent principals), and `agent_sandbox` (all player/crew control leases belong to AI agents; humans may spectate and optionally advise). Use `agent_sandbox` as an opt-in world type and a useful stress/evaluation environment, not as the only way to play. In this mode models drive actor choices, communication, planning, and social behavior; the authoritative game still calculates physics, materials, construction, environment, economy, events, access, and outcomes in deterministic, versioned simulation code. This keeps an LLM-driven world legible and replayable without pretending generated text is state.

Provide a versioned bring-your-own-agent API with OpenAPI/JSON Schema contracts. A registered `AgentPrincipal` maps to one or more explicitly delegated player/character control leases. Agents request **scoped observations** containing only the state that the embodied actor may perceive or access, then submit typed intents to a bounded queue. Return an accepted/queued ticket immediately and publish the applied/rejected result through the next observation, event stream, or status endpoint; the region tick rechecks identity, lease, permissions, capabilities, access, range, resources, world revision, and interlocks before applying each intent. Include idempotency keys, replay protection, rate/concurrency/body-size limits, token rotation and revocation, model/provider attribution, loop detection, and audit history. No endpoint permits direct database writes or arbitrary simulation code.

Keep PHP agent services separate and PSR-4 compatible: `packages/php/agent-contracts/` owns versioned principals, observation/intent/result DTOs, schemas, and fixtures; `apps/php-agent-gateway/` handles agent auth, scoped observations, intent intake/dispatch, and status; `apps/php-agent-runner/` hosts optional observe/decide/act loops and adapters such as DiscordPHP bots and local/remote LLMs. Use an async Promise-based API/service contract: model inference, Discord messages, and agent calls run outside the Godot physics/tick loop, and completions enqueue proposals that the region authority later revalidates. The agent runner may be offline or paused without blocking world simulation; define whether a sandbox world pauses its agent-controlled crew, lets deterministic safety controllers hold stable states, or continues only non-player systems when its agents cannot respond.

Humans in an `agent_sandbox` world are observers/advisers, not hidden player controllers. Advice delivered through Discord or the Social SDK is untrusted context that agents may consider; it is never a command, permission grant, or way to bypass the agent intent API. Admins retain separate, permission-gated operational controls with an audit trail. Agent identity must be distinguishable from human identity in UI, logs, market actions, voice/chat, and analytics. Keep personal prompts private according to the provider setting and never expose a player/agent token to another agent or model.

Use [No Human Allowed (NHA-MMO)](https://github.com/Recluse/nha-mmo) as a design reference for all-agent actors, human spectator/adviser roles, curated observations, queued typed intents, tick-applied outcomes, loop guards, and replayable audit history. Use the project-owned [DiscordPHP-NHA](https://github.com/Valgorithms/DiscordPHP-NHA) as a first-party implementation reference for a DiscordPHP API client, typed action helpers, observation/status rendering, Ollama `AgentBrain`, observe/decide/act autoplay, and autonomous Discord relay patterns. The latter repository is MIT-licensed and currently pinned in the local checkout at commit `ca8ddb3ed44fcf18887af143e7cdeba39d2a42b1`; record the reviewed commit and any changed working-tree source separately before reuse. Port concepts behind the game's agent contracts rather than coupling Godot authority to DiscordPHP or the NHA API. Audit exact third-party files, assets, and transitive dependencies for MIT-compatible rights before copying; a project's top-level MIT license does not automatically license its dependencies or external world content.

### Future NPC crew and colony simulation

Use RimWorld as a **gameplay reference** for emergent colony stories: needs and wellbeing, skills, schedules, prioritized work, relationships, faction context, and incidents can make a small crew feel distinct without authored quest scripting for every situation. Design an original data model and UI around the project's own engineering and roleplay loops. Do not reuse RimWorld code, art, audio, writing, interface layouts, names, or other game content; Ludeon's FAQ says RimWorld assets are for RimWorld mods. ([Ludeon: RimWorld FAQ](https://ludeon.com/blog/faq/))

Implement NPC behavior as server-owned, task-based planning over the same work orders, blueprint construction sites, device capabilities, access checks, interaction commands, and safety interlocks available to players. Model needs, skills, work preferences, schedules, relationships, and faction goals as separate components/data, with policies that can be tuned per world. A job board can turn player-authored plans and world conditions into work candidates; an NPC planner scores eligible jobs, reserves inputs, navigates to the work, revalidates requirements on arrival, and reports completion or a typed reason for failure. Higher-level needs and social choices can update less often than movement and immediate reactions. Use event-driven interruption and bounded catch-up for inactive regions instead of running every NPC at full rate while nobody is present.

Keep routine NPC decision-making deterministic and independent of LLM services. The onboard LLM can converse, summarize status, help a player draft a program, or propose a permitted command through the existing tool/ACL boundary; it should not be necessary for an NPC to eat, maintain a schedule, choose a repair job, or obey its faction rules. Add NPCs only after the authoritative work-order and blueprint systems are stable, beginning with one crew member who can complete a constrained maintenance task. This keeps inference cost and provider outages from becoming prerequisites for the persistent world.

## Make assets, validation, and scope auditable

### Open asset policy that fits the art constraint

Use an **MIT-first contribution policy**. Every first-party work—code, documentation, shaders, scripts, procedural asset generators and source, original art/audio, blueprint schemas, sample blueprints, and AI-assisted contributions—must be cleared for, and released by the project under, MIT. ### Validation strategy and release gates

Test subsystem rules without rendering or Discord. Unit tests should cover typed units, pure domain-solver inputs/deltas/events, composed device capabilities, stable IDs and schema migration, power/pressure state transitions, failure conditions, program parsing and budgets, and persistence migrations. Blueprint tests should validate required fields and version migrations, part/edge graph integrity, connector and unit compatibility, structural/resource constraints, construction stage/material invariants, capability-registry lookup, invalid or oversized imports, and round-trip serialization. Material tests should cover deposit grade and depletion, extraction yields, batch identity/provenance, process input/output schemas, element/mass balance and rounding tolerances, contamination and byproduct accounting, energy/time/tool constraints, recycling and salvage recovery, and restart/migration of reserved batches and active jobs. Prove that repeated processing cannot create free matter and that every irrecoverable fraction is represented as a loss/byproduct. Prove that a user-provided definition cannot add executable code, widen actor/device permissions, or bypass server validation. Build the same small airlock/power/storage assembly through the blueprint pipeline and verify placement, construction, operation, save/reload, and repair. The initial pressure suite should cover the simplified compartment/airlock model; detailed mixture and thermodynamics tests belong to the later fidelity gate. Multiplayer integration tests should exercise forged RPC inputs, unauthorized IDs, stale preconditions, duplicate requests, late join, reconnect, command races, and rate limits. Persistence tests should save, crash/restart, reload, and prove the same durable state and program revisions recover. Use synthetic latency, packet loss, and reordering; measure tick time, memory, bandwidth, save duration, and crash recovery as entity counts grow. Define a player/concurrency budget only after representative loads exist.

Ship-assembly tests should validate grid/anchor placement, attachment and docking ports, component compatibility, mass/center-of-mass and thrust envelopes, power/fuel/thermal/pressure networks, construction resource consumption, and blueprint-diff refits. Exercise removing or replacing modules while crew, cargo, programs, and connected services exist; changes must preserve stable IDs and force an explicit safe migration of anything affected. Simulate internal faults and exterior hull damage, verify access/isolation/tool/suit/tether requirements, repair materials and progress, and prove that a ship can be upgraded, damaged, repaired, unloaded, transferred between regions, and restored after restart. Add shipyard tests for duplicate job submission, conflicting berths, exact material batch reservations, insufficient/contaminated feedstock, queue stage transitions, cancellation/recovery, authorized AI scopes, delegated budgets, provider offline behavior, and commissioning approvals. Add load scenarios at increasing blocks/modules per ship and concurrent yard jobs to compare physics/network/save/queue cost against profiling budgets.

Procedural-world tests should assert identical generated manifests for the same world/target/profile/generator versions; unique IDs and valid bounded resources/threats; safe schema migration when generator versions change; and no silent regeneration of a materialized target. Test discovery, travel-time activation, empty-instance unload, unvisited candidate expiry, active-crew timer pause/extension, explicit salvage/claim completion, disconnect/reconnect, and archival while preserving player construction, inventory, rewards, contract status, and durable history. Validate generated links, schema references, examples, and retrieval chunks, then rebuild twice to prove stable output. Review generated diffs for clarity; automated completeness cannot establish that explanations are understandable to a new player. Test that the in-game manual can resolve a device to its matching versioned entry and that an LLM documentation lookup returns only approved documentation and authorized live state, never a permission grant.

Use a fake LLM provider for malformed tool calls, repeated calls, prompt-injection fixtures, timeout, cancellation, quotas, loop detection, and action approval replay. Add provider conformance tests for Ollama and each later adapter. Agent API tests cover world-mode policy, agent/character lease binding, human observer/adviser restrictions, visibility-filtered observations, typed intent validation, idempotency and replay, queue status/result delivery, tick-side revalidation, rate limits, token rotation/revocation, audit/replay, and provider timeout/offline behavior without tick stalls. Verify that agents cannot bypass ordinary interaction, ACL, module capability, law, safety, material reservation, or shipyard job rules. In `agent_sandbox`, prove that only registered agent principals control crew and human advice never directly mutates the world. Contract-test versioned PHP/Godot payloads with shared fixtures; test PHP transaction rollback, idempotency, migrations, and persistence outage behavior without a running game. The key end-to-end gate is two players in one habitat: one authors a pressure-control program, the server compiles and runs it through safety checks, both clients observe the same durable result, a restart preserves its source, and a revoked role stops its commands. Social tests separately cover Discord login/link/unlink, lobby lifecycle, denied access, voice failure, moderation webhook acknowledgement, and rate-limit recovery. Steam tests cover valid/invalid/replayed tickets, app/ownership mismatch, player_id linking, secret absence from client exports, friend invite/join resolution to a canonical world ID, lobby teardown without world loss, stats/achievement authority, and SteamGameServer initialization in a genuinely headless environment. The transport spike compares ENet and SteamNetworkingSockets/SDR under the same scripted load. Event Director tests cover random scheduling disabled at master/category/event levels; zero-weight exclusion from random selection; cooldown and global/per-event concurrency caps; rejection of invalid or stale causal triggers; admin preview without mutation, ACL denial, reason/audit logging, confirm/cancel, and immediate emergency shutdown; persistence of seed, parameters, and lifecycle through restart; and an empty offline colony that pauses or resolves safely without a permanent wipe. Simulated Discord or Steam outages must not corrupt simulation state. Native SDK packaging gets a smoke test per target platform.

Keep future NPC tests in the same authoritative domain: a small test colonist should receive a work order, check access, skills, path/interaction preconditions, and consume the same materials and machine APIs as a player. Test deterministic task choice for fixed inputs, interruption/replanning, offline catch-up bounds, relationship/faction policy, and save/reload. NPC routine behavior must continue with no LLM configured; LLM interaction is an optional bounded tool layer, never a source of NPC authority. Add embodiment tests for atomic brain-to-cyborg and clone transfers, old-body control revocation, one-active-body enforcement after reconnect/races, implant/clone failure and restart recovery, module capability allow/deny cases, lawset conflict resolution and audit, station-AI network reachability, delegated-agent takeover, and safe idle behavior when an AI provider is unavailable.

### Risks to actively manage

| Risk | Early signal | Mitigation and gate |
|---|---|---|
| Ambition outpaces a small team | Work expands into planets, vehicles, and systems before the habitat loop is stable. | Freeze features behind the roadmap gates; ship one complete site and subsystem at a time. |
| Social SDK access or quotas delay launch | Communication access is denied, quotas stay low, or platform support differs. | Apply early; prototype the C++ bridge; keep world identity, persistence, and play independent of Discord; confirm caps before product commitments. |
| Steamworks identity or headless integration is brittle | Tickets fail in dedicated deployments, credentials reach client exports, or lobby joins become tied to host availability. | Build php-steam-auth early; verify tickets server-side; keep secrets in PHP; test SteamGameServer initialization separately; map lobbies to canonical world IDs only. |
| Steam platform terms conflict with the open-source stack | A dependency license or SDK redistribution condition blocks Steam distribution. | Keep GodotSteam wrapper license separate from Steamworks terms; perform dependency/license review against Valve's current rules before release and use permissive dependencies where possible. |
| Transport choice couples persistence to a lobby | A peer host or lobby disappears and the shared colony becomes unavailable or inconsistent. | Keep dedicated authoritative regions; benchmark ENet against SteamNetworkingSockets/SDR behind a transport adapter; treat Steam lobbies as discovery only. |
| Native extension becomes a maintenance bottleneck | Packaging or callback handling breaks across OS/platform builds. | Keep a thin bridge, pin SDK/compiler versions, automate per-platform smoke checks, and isolate the GDScript-facing interface. |
| AI automation becomes an exploit surface | Model follows malicious signage/chat or scripts execute excessive work. | Use a bounded DSL/VM, complete mediation, tool allowlists, budgets, confirmation hashes, kill switches, and adversarial fixtures. |
| Persistence or scaling is guessed too early | State loss, slow saves, late-join spikes, or CPU stalls appear with more entities. | Separate persistent domain records from nodes; snapshot plus journal; measure before process sharding and publish restore/load acceptance budgets. |
| Blueprint format becomes a second runtime or an unsafe mod API | Definitions duplicate simulation rules, migrations break saves, or imported files introduce executable behavior. | Keep definitions declarative and versioned; route all capabilities through trusted registries; validate graph/ports/resources and migrations; prove one shared blueprint from authoring through restore before expanding freeform scope. |
| Material processing becomes a combinatorial or exploitable crafting system | Recipes multiply without readable feedback, matter appears/disappears, or recycling creates infinite value. | Model typed process graphs and batch properties; assert mass/element balances, losses/byproducts, energy/tool limits, and process-version migrations; expand the material catalog only with a tested gameplay need. |
| LLM/agent usage creates unplanned service bills | Remote providers are called too often, autoplay loops waste tokens, or a hosted agent sandbox scales cost with actor count. | Default to local Ollama or deterministic non-LLM behavior; require remote opt-in and per-world/per-agent budgets, cooldowns, concurrency caps, and usage visibility; profile local hardware and inference cadence before hosted commitments. |
| PHP calls leak into real-time ticks | Tick stalls or region simulation depends on network availability for routine movement and device updates. | Keep the physical tick in Godot; use PHP for control-plane/business services and bounded durable checkpoints or explicit lifecycle calls with contracts, idempotency, timeout, and safe-mode behavior. |
| Incidents become griefing or wipe unattended colonies | Hazard effects persist while empty, admin requests skip notice, or event recovery loses its seed/state. | Use opt-in/bounded event rules, telegraphs, server-side ACL/eligibility checks, stored lifecycle/audit records, emergency shutdown, and safe offline pause/recovery; never default random incidents to permanent colony destruction. |
| Large-world precision breaks interaction | Jitter, physics instability, or placement drift increases with distance. | Use region-local origins and logical coordinates; prove local range before considering large-world builds. |
| Free assets have unclear rights | Search results omit item licenses, or AI output provenance is unavailable. | Require file-level provenance and human approval; prefer MIT-licensed original work or CC0 imports; keep uncertain material out of shipped builds. |
| Engineering realism becomes unreadable | Players cannot see why a system failed or which actions matter. | Use typed diagrams, alarms, logs, explanatory diagnostics, and training scenarios; validate mechanics through playable repairs. |
| Documentation drifts or LLM context is misleading | UI help, manuals, schemas, and model answers disagree or reference removed assets. | Generate inventories from versioned source metadata; require docs in feature acceptance; validate links/schema IDs and regenerated diffs; keep the server and executable contracts authoritative for actions. |
| Profiling perturbs the workload or exposes sensitive data | Instrumented runs look slower than real play, profile queues grow, or captures include account/prompt/content details. | Keep baseline counters cheap, sample detailed traces, test overhead, bound buffers and retention, drop diagnostic samples rather than block, redact sensitive fields, and gate profile access/upload. |
| Asset sales distract from the game or conflict with MIT | A listing depends on unreleased gameplay, adds a second support burden, implies exclusivity, or marketplace terms obscure user rights. | Productize only validated reusable work; keep full MIT source and provenance public; state the license plainly; check current Godot/marketplace sales terms and fees; stop or defer listings that miss the productization gate. |

## Conclusion

The architecture makes the long horizon achievable by making every expansion reuse compact, testable, documented, and profiled rules: region-local physics, stable world records, versioned blueprints, conserved material batches, reusable processes, composable assemblies and bodies, server-owned commands, typed machine networks, permission-scoped automation, and generated references for PHP services and Godot content. The first milestone should prove that a small crew can keep one habitat alive, extract and refine a local resource, manufacture and then recycle a useful part, and build from the same blueprint contract intended for later ships and stations. Later ship refits, orbital yards, exterior repairs, procedural regions, and salvage expeditions reuse those contracts. Agent-sandbox worlds, NPCs, cyborgs, and Station AI also use the same authoritative action and simulation systems, with LLMs choosing actions instead of rewriting world rules.

Discord, Steam, and LLMs are valuable force multipliers when treated as adapters around the game’s own authority. The paid official release is both a commercial game and a flagship advertisement for the open-source ecosystem: purchasing it should support continued work on DiscordPHP and other projects under a published, reportable allocation policy. The source can stay MIT-licensed; success depends on a polished game, a clear purchase-to-funding message, and dependable official services, not on preventing others from reusing the code.
